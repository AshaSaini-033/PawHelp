# AI discussion 02 — Could PawHelp work around NIT Srinagar and Kashmir?

**Purpose:** Think through local feasibility before spending time building features.

**Evidence status:** This is a preliminary context assessment based on the project discussion. It is not a survey of NIT Srinagar, not an official campus-policy check, and not proof that feeders or volunteers are available. The student must verify each point through local observation and a conversation.

## 1. Is the idea practical in this setting?

**Potentially, as a small pilot—not automatically as a campus-wide service.**

A practical first test would cover one compact area that the student can safely visit on foot around the campus or a nearby community. The pilot should involve one regular feeder/coordinator and a small number of confirmed feeding points.

The app is most useful if the feeder says that food supply, missed check-ins, or finding a willing helper is genuinely difficult. If feeding is already reliably coordinated through an existing group, a new app may add little value.

## 2. Local factors that could affect adoption

| Local factor to investigate | How it could affect PawHelp | What to verify in person |
|---|---|---|
| Winter weather, cold, snow or icy paths | Volunteers may not be able to reach an outdoor point at the planned time | Which routes and locations remain safely accessible in bad weather? |
| Walking distance and terrain | A nearby offer may still be inconvenient to collect or deliver | Which points are actually within a realistic walking distance? |
| Student timetable and exams | Student availability may change sharply by hour, weekday or exam period | When are students genuinely free, and who can help outside class hours? |
| Campus and hostel rules | Feeding, storage, movement or volunteer activity may need permission | Are there campus rules or a designated contact person? Do not assume permission. |
| Existing feeders and local groups | An existing informal network may already coordinate visits effectively | How do feeders currently communicate, and what part of the process fails? |
| Food availability and storage | Donated food may be unavailable, unsuitable or difficult to store safely | Which food types does the experienced feeder approve, and how are they stored? |
| Community acceptance | Nearby residents or campus users may have concerns about feeding locations | Are there agreed locations and practices that avoid conflict or obstruction? |
| Animal-welfare guidance | Feeding practices must be safe and locally appropriate | Ask an experienced feeder or relevant animal-welfare professional; do not use app suggestions as veterinary advice. |
| Mobile connectivity and device access | A web app may not be convenient for every feeder or volunteer | Does the intended person have reliable internet and want to use a web dashboard? |
| Privacy and safety | Publicly sharing exact spots or personal details may create risks | Which area labels can be shared, and which details must stay private? |

## 3. What would make a Kashmir/NIT pilot fail?

- There is no regular feeder willing to maintain accurate records.
- People already coordinate successfully and do not want another tool.
- Weather or distance makes collection and delivery impractical.
- Students are available only irregularly, so a listed availability window quickly becomes stale.
- Campus permission or community acceptance is not obtained where needed.
- Food offers cannot be checked for suitability, quantity or freshness.
- The project exposes exact locations or personal information without consent.

## 4. What would make a small pilot worth trying?

- One feeder confirms a specific coordination problem.
- The first pilot area is compact and safely reachable.
- At least a few real feeding-point, food-offer and volunteer records can be collected with permission.
- People agree to confirm availability and outcomes rather than treating an app suggestion as a commitment.
- The tool saves the feeder time or makes an existing gap easier to see.

## 5. Suggested field validation around NIT Srinagar

Before claiming local feasibility:

1. Identify one regular feeder or coordinator and ask permission to speak with them.
2. Ask how feeding points, food, and helpers are currently coordinated.
3. Ask what happens when the usual feeder is unavailable or food is not available.
4. Ask whether weather, walking distance, campus rules or storage cause real difficulties.
5. Walk only routes that are safe and permitted. Record broad area labels rather than private addresses.
6. Collect the assignment's required first-hand records yourself. Do not fill the CSV with estimates or generated examples.
7. Ask the feeder to review the proposed workflow and tell you what would be unnecessary or wrong.

## 6. Conclusion

PawHelp could be tested as a **small local coordination board** around one confirmed area. Kashmir's weather, accessibility, campus rules, existing community networks and volunteer schedules may materially affect whether it is useful. These are factors to investigate, not claims that the app has already solved them.

The right question is not “Can this app cover all of Kashmir?” It is “Can this simple workflow help one real feeder in one small area, without creating extra work or unsafe expectations?”
